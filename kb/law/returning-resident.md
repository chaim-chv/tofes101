# תושב חוזר — returning resident

## 1. Why this is messy

“תושב חוזר” is used in three different senses:

1. **s. 35(d) definition** — a person who returned and became an Israeli resident
   in a narrow historical window (16.5.2010–30.9.2012) after six consecutive years
   abroad, or who holds a returning-resident certificate from the Ministry of
   Aliyah showing six years abroad. This definition feeds the **oleh credit** in
   s. 35.
2. **Administrative “returning resident” track** — the Tax Authority/Ministry of
   Aliyah recognise additional returning-resident categories (e.g. “תושב חוזר
   ותיק”, after 10 years abroad) with their own exemption/credit rules.
3. **2026 temporary law** — “תושבים חוזרים ותיקים” who arrive between 5.11.2025
   and 31.12.2026 get the new income-tax exemption (see [[law/new-immigrant]]).

The 2026 form 101 has **no separate “returning resident” checkbox**; item 4 is
labeled “אני עולה חדש/ה” and requires a תעודת עולה. A returning resident who is
treated as an oleh under s. 35 may use item 4 with the appropriate certificate;
others must go to the assessor / use the dedicated service.

## 2. What is verified

- s. 35(d) includes “תושב חוזר” in the definition of “עולה” for the credit.
- The 2026 temporary exemption expressly covers “תושבים חוזרים ותיקים” arriving
  in the window, with the same ceilings as olim (600k in 2026, 1m in 2027–28).
- The 2026 immigrant guide describes the oleh/returning-resident credit schedule
  (1/3/2/1 over 54 months for 2022+).
- The 101’s item 4 text and document list are oleh-specific.

## 3. What is not verified / must be routed

- The exact eligibility threshold for “תושב חוזר ותיק” (years abroad, age,
  employment, etc.) and whether it is claimed on the 101 or only in the annual
  return.
- Whether a returning resident who does not meet s. 35(d) can tick item 4; the
  form says “עולה”, and the electronic matrix only enforces a תעודת עולה.
- The interaction between the 2026 temporary exemption and the s. 35 points for
  a person who arrives in 2026 and is also a “תושב חוזר ותיק”.

## 4. Practical rule for the app

1. If the user says “I am a returning resident”, do **not** silently tick item 4.
2. Ask for the document: תעודת עולה / אישור תושב חוזר from the Ministry of
   Aliyah / a tax-authority approval.
3. If the document is a תעודת עולה or the user meets s. 35(d), allow item 4 and
   compute the oleh schedule.
4. Otherwise, show a “requires assessor / dedicated returning-resident service”
   message and offer form 116 / the online coordination service.
5. For the 2026 temporary exemption, link to the Tax Authority’s oleh/returning
   resident service; do not compute the exemption in the app without the circular.

## 5. Why the distinction matters financially

- A returning resident treated as an oleh can get up to 8.5 annual points over 54
  months plus the 2.25 base; a person on the ordinary track gets only the base.
- The 2026 temporary exemption can be worth far more than the points for a
  high earner (600k ceiling in 2026), but it is income-based, not a point.
- Wrongly ticking item 4 without a certificate is a false declaration; the employer
  may apply a credit the employee is not entitled to.

## 6. Sources and confidence

The statutory text and the 2026 guide are verified; the administrative
returning-resident tracks and the exact 101 treatment are `likely`/unverified.
This node deliberately does not invent a threshold or a formula.
