# נקודות זיכוי — the complete map

## 1. What a credit point is

1. A **credit point** (נקודת זיכוי) is a fixed annual NIS amount subtracted from
   the income tax otherwise due. It is defined in s. 33א of the Income Tax
   Ordinance and is **not** a refundable benefit.
2. For 2025 and 2026 one point = **2,904 ₪/year = 242 ₪/month**. The value is
   frozen for 2024–2027 by the 2025 budget freeze law
   ([[source/taxes-monthly-deductions-2026]] p. 3, 12).
3. A point reduces **tax**, not income. If the employee’s tax liability is lower
   than the points’ value, the tax floor is zero; the unused balance does not
   become a cash payment. It can offset tax on other income in the annual return
   and generate a refund of over-withheld tax.
4. The employer applies 1/12 of the annual value each month. The form 101 collects
   the **facts/claims**; it does not contain a “number of points” field — payroll
   computes the number.

## 2. The base every Israeli resident gets

| Track | Points | Statute | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Resident of Israel | **2.00** | s. 34 | “תושב ישראל” in the tax year |
| Travel to work | **0.25** | s. 36 | the “זיכוי נסיעה” component; together with s. 34 this is the familiar **2.25** |
| Woman | **+0.50** | s. 36א | total 2.75 for a female resident |

A non-resident does **not** get the 2.25; foreign workers have a separate regime
([[law/residency-and-foreign-workers]]).

## 5. Exemptions that are **not** points

| Track | What it is | Statute | Node |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100% disability / permanent blindness | exemption of income up to a ceiling, not a credit | s. 9(5) | [[law/disability-exemption]] |
| Settlement (יישוב מזכה) | tax benefit/credit/exemption under s. 11, rates vary by locality | s. 11 | [[law/form-101-section-map]] item 3 |
| Foreign resident / foreign worker | separate withholding and point regime | regulations | [[law/residency-and-foreign-workers]] |

Do not convert an exemption into “points” in the UI. Item 2 of section ח is an
exemption; its value depends on the income ceiling and the employee’s other income.

## 6. How the employer applies the points

1. Start from the annual tax on the projected income (2026 brackets).
2. Subtract the annual value of the points the employee claimed and proved.
3. Divide by 12 for the monthly withholding.
4. If the employee has other income and uses the points elsewhere, this employer
   withholds at the maximum rate (section ה item 8).
5. If the employee started mid-year with no prior income, section ט item 1 allows
   the full monthly credit from day one; otherwise the annual return reconciles.

## 7. Part-year and change rules

- Most personal credits are **annual** and are not prorated by birth month (a child
  born in December gives the full birth-year credit).
- Marital status changes are prorated by months in the year of change (s. 41 for
  a spouse who was married part of the year; the single-parent/divorce tracks
  similarly look at the months of eligibility).
- Aliyah credits are prorated by months of residence in Israel in the tax year.
- Soldier/reserve credits are monthly fractions by design.
- A change that ends eligibility (child turns 18, spouse starts earning above the
  threshold, employee ceases to be a resident) must be reflected from the change,
  with the annual return correcting the year.

## 8. The “credit ceiling” question

There is no general annual cap on the **number** of personal credit points. The
practical ceiling is the tax liability: points cannot reduce tax below zero. The
main explicit ceilings in the 101 context are:

- the **exemption ceilings** for disability/blindness (684,000 / 445,200 / 81,960
  in 2025–2026);
- the **surtax** threshold (721,560) — not a credit ceiling;
- the specific caps on reserve credit (4 points) and on the number of years for
  study/soldier credits.

## 9. Common implementation errors

1. Using 2,904 ₪/month instead of /year (the monthly value is 242).
2. Applying 2.25 to a non-resident.
3. Giving the woman’s 0.5 to a male employee or to a female non-resident.
4. Applying the father’s 1 point for age 6–17 to the mother (she gets 2).
5. Giving the 0.5 point at age 18 to the father (only the mother gets it).
6. Applying the oleh 3-point rate for all 54 months (it steps down).
7. Treating the disability exemption as 2.25 points.
8. Forgetting that the spouse credit requires retirement age/disability, not just
   “no income”.
9. Applying the single-parent +1 twice (items 6 and 9 are alternatives).
10. Using the 2025 20%/31% thresholds for 2026.
