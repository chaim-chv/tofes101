# Alimony credit

## 1. The rule

A **divorced person who has remarried** and who (or whose spouse) pays alimony to
the **former spouse** gets **1 point** (s. 40א).

- It starts in the **year of the remarriage** and is granted for the **full year**
  (not prorated).
- It applies to alimony paid to a **former spouse**, not to child support.
- It is available in addition to the per-child points and the single-parent points.
- It is claimed in form 101 section ח item 12.

## 2. What it is not

| Not this | Correct track |
| --- | --- |
| Child support to children from a previous marriage | children points / single-parent item 10 |
| Alimony paid by a person who has **not** remarried | no s. 40א point (the divorce/single-parent tracks may apply) |
| Support paid to a current spouse | no point; the dependent/working-spouse rules apply |
| A payment that is not court-ordered/agreed | the assessor may require a judgment/agreement |

## 3. Documents

- Divorce judgment / support judgment (or a written agreement approved by the
  court/assessor).
- Proof of the remarriage (the form’s marital status = נשוי/אה + spouse details).
- The form’s item 12 says “ימולא ע״י מי שנישא בשנית. מצורף פסק דין”.

## 4. Interaction with other tracks

- The +1 is **not** the same as the single-parent +1 (item 6/9); a remarried
  parent may lose item 6 but keep item 12.
- A non-custodial parent paying child support may get item 10 (1 point or part)
  and, if remarried and paying spousal alimony, item 12 (+1) — different payments,
  different points.
- The credit is against the tax on all income, not only personal effort.

## 5. Implementation

1. Enable item 12 only if marital status = נשוי/אה (remarried).
2. Require a divorce/support judgment.
3. Do not ask for the amount of alimony; the point is fixed at 1, not proportional.
4. Do not prorate by months; the statute grants it for the full year of remarriage.
5. If the employee divorced again, the point stops when the obligation/judgment
   ends; verify the current judgment.

## 6. Unverified

  recognition order; route to the assessor.
- The treatment of a same-sex former spouse under the current wording; the form is
  gendered. Legal advice required.
