# Retention

## 1. The period

Under the bookkeeping rules (הוראות מס הכנסה (ניהול פנקסי חשבונות), תשל״ג-1973,
s. 25(ג)):

> keep the accounting system for **7 years from the end of the tax year**, or
> **6 years from the date the annual return was filed**, **whichever is later**.

For a payroll record such as form 101, the practical rule is **at least 7 years**
from the end of the relevant tax year. If the employer files the annual return
late, the 6-years-from-filing alternative can extend the period; take the later of
the two.

Section 25(ד) sets a **3-year** minimum for other documents the business keeps but
is not required to keep as part of the accounting system. Form 101 is payroll
evidence, so do not rely on the 3-year minimum; use 7 years.

## 2. What to keep

- The signed form (paper original or locked PDF).
- All attachments: ID + ספח, passport + visa, oleh certificate, NII disability
  approval, form 1312א, discharge certificate, form 119, divorce/support judgment,
  assessor approval for “פרוד/ה”, coordination approval.
- The employee’s confirmation/declaration (electronic).
- **Every version** of the form, including previous years and mid-year updates
  (2026 circular §ב.2.י–ב.2.יא).
- The employer’s validation/approval record and the signature date.
- Form 106 (annual statement) and form 126 (annual report) are separate records but
  part of the same payroll file.

## 3. Digital archive requirements

The 2026 circular requires the electronic form, attachments and employee
confirmation to be stored in a **computerized archive** that allows access and
retrieval when needed, including all previous versions. This means:

- no “overwrite” of a previous version;
- a retrievable audit trail (who changed what, when);
- the locked PDF + attachments together;
- access controls and backup.

## 4. Privacy tension

- Retention is a **legal obligation**, so it overrides a generic “delete my data”
  request for the final form; but the employer should delete drafts, duplicates and
  data no longer needed.
- Do not keep the data longer than the retention period; after it, delete or
  anonymise.
- Do not use the retained data for marketing or share it with advertisers.
- The employee can request access/correction under the Privacy Protection Law; the
  employer must respond within the statutory framework.

## 5. Practical policy

1. Store by tax year and employee ID (access-controlled).
2. Set a retention clock at the end of the tax year; do not delete before the
   later of 7 years / 6 years from filing.
3. On update, create a new version; never overwrite.
4. On termination, keep the file for the same period; give the employee form 106.
5. On audit, produce the form + attachments + versions.
6. On expiry, secure deletion (including backups, subject to backup rotation).

## 6. Unverified

- Whether a specific payroll record is classified as “מערכת החשבונות” or “תיעוד”
  under s. 25; the conservative 7-year rule is the safe implementation.
- Any sector-specific retention rule longer than 7 years (e.g. public-sector
  archives); check the employer’s sector.
