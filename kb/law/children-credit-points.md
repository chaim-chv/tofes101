# Children credit points

## 1. The rule in one table

For each child, per tax year (2024–2026 schedule):

| Child’s age in the tax year | Mother / custodial parent | Father / non-custodial parent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Year of birth | **2.5** | **2.5** |
| 1–2 | **4.5** | **4.5** |
| 3 | **3.5** | **3.5** |
| 4–5 | **2.5** | **2.5** |
| 6–17 | **2.0** | **1.0** |
| 18 (year of majority) | **0.5** | **0** |

The difference between the parents is only in the 6–18 bands: the mother gets 2
points for ages 6–17 and 0.5 at 18; the father gets 1 point for 6–17 and nothing
at 18. The under-6 bands are the same for both parents from 2024.

Statutory basis: s. 40(b) (single-parent family and the father’s parallel track)
and s. 66(c)(4)/(5) (the mother’s and father’s points on a separate computation).
The table is confirmed by the Tax Authority’s 2026 parents page and Kol Zchut.

## 2. Pre-2024 values (do not use for 2025/2026)

| Age | 2017–2023 |
| --- | --- |
| Birth | 1.5 |
| 1–5 | 2.5 |
| 6–12 | 1 (from 2022; 0 before) |
| 13–17 | 1 (from 2024; 0 before) |
| 18 | 0.5 (mother) |

The 2024 reform added 1 point per parent for ages 0–3 and extended the 6–17 point
to ages 13–17. Applying the old table to 2025/2026 under-credits the employee.

## 3. How to determine the age band

1. Use the child’s age **at the end of the tax year** (31 December), or
   equivalently the year in which the birthday falls.
2. “Year of birth” = the tax year in which the child was born, regardless of the
   month. A December baby still gives the full 2.5 for that year.
3. “Age 1–2” = the tax years in which the child turns 1 or 2.
4. “Age 18” = the tax year in which the child turns 18 (the year of majority).
5. The form lists children who are **under 19** in the tax year. A child who
   turns 19 during the year is not listed, even if the birthday is in December.
6. Do not prorate the child credit by birth month. The only proration in this
   area is the single-parent/part-year marital status rule (months of eligibility).

## 4. Who claims which track

The form has two distinct child blocks:

| Form item | Label | Who | Schedule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ח 7 | “בגין ילדי שבחזקתי המפורטים בחלק ג” | mother; custodial parent; single parent receiving the NII child allowance | mother/custodial: 2.5 / 4.5 / 3.5 / 2.5 / 2 / 0.5 |
| ח 8 | “בגין ילדיי המפורטים בחלק ג” | father; parent whose children are not in their custody; a parent who is not the “הורה במשפחה חד הורית” | father/non-custodial: 2.5 / 4.5 / 3.5 / 2.5 / 1 / — |

The electronic matrix enforces the counters: item 7 has six age bands including
18; item 8 has five bands and no 18. The employer compares the counters with the
child rows in section ג.

## 5. Both parents, married or not

- **Married/registered couple**: each parent claims their own schedule in their
  own payroll (the mother item 7, the father item 8). The under-6 points are
  granted to both from 2024; this is not a double claim of the same point.
- **Separated/divorced**: the parent with custody (usually the mother) claims item
  7; the other parent claims item 8 if the child is not in their custody. The
  non-custodial parent may also get the +1 “participates in the child’s upkeep”
  point (item 10) if they pay support — see [[law/single-parent-credit]].
- **Single parent (“הורה יחיד”)**: item 9 adds +1 once, on top of the per-child
  schedule, where the other parent is dead or absent from the population registry.
- **Same-sex/other family forms**: the form only offers male/female and
  married/single/divorced/widowed/separated; the statute speaks in terms of
  mother/father. Do not invent a third track; route unusual cases to the assessor.
  **Unverified** how payroll should map a same-sex registered couple; legal advice
  is required.

## 6. Child allowance and custody (section ג)

- Column 1 = “הילד נמצא בחזקתי” (the child is in my custody). It is enabled only
  after name, ID and birth date are filled.
- Column 2 = “אני מקבל/ת בגינו קצבת ילדים מב״ל” (I receive NII child allowance for
  this child). It is enabled only after column 1.
- The NII child allowance is not the tax credit; it is evidence of the custody/
  support arrangement used to decide who claims item 7.
- The form does not ask for the amount of the allowance or the other parent’s ID.

## 7. Interaction with other tracks

- A child with a disability can generate **2 points** under s. 45 in addition to
  the age-based points; see [[law/disabled-child-credit]].
- A child for whom one parent pays support but who is not in their custody can
  generate the non-custodial +1/part point under s. 40(b)(2); see
  [[law/single-parent-credit]].
- A child who is 18 in the tax year: the mother gets 0.5, the father 0; the child
  is still listed if under 19 at the end of the year.
- A child who is 19+ is outside the 101 child table; the parents may still have
  other tracks (e.g. disabled adult child) but not the age-based points.

## 8. Worked examples (2026)

### Example A — married couple, children aged 2 and 8

- Mother: 4.5 (age 2) + 2 (age 8) = **6.5** child points; plus 2.25 resident +
  0.5 woman = **9.25** total.
- Father: 4.5 (age 2) + 1 (age 8) = **5.5** child points; plus 2.25 = **7.75**
  total.
- Both parents claim their own under-6 point for the 2-year-old; this is the
  2024 reform, not a duplicate.

### Example B — divorced mother, child aged 8, father pays support

- Mother (custody): item 7 → 2 points for age 8; plus item 6 (single-parent living
  apart) → +1; plus 2.25 + 0.5 = **5.75** total.
- Father (non-custody): item 8 → 1 point for age 8; plus item 10 (participates in
  upkeep, with a support judgment) → +1 or part; plus 2.25 = **4.25** (or the
  agreed fraction).

### Example C — “הורה יחיד” (other parent deceased), child aged 8

- Parent: item 7 → 2 points; item 9 → +1; plus 2.25 (+0.5 if female) = **5.25**
  (male) or **5.75** (female).

## 9. Implementation checks

1. Build the age band from the tax year and the birth date, not from “today”.
2. Store the child’s ID as a string with leading zeros.
3. Validate the child ID with the same checksum as the employee ID; do not require
   it if the child has no Israeli ID (the form is ID-based; foreign children are
   an edge case).
4. Reject a child row that is partially filled: name, ID and birth date are
   all-or-nothing.
5. Do not allow the same child to appear twice.
6. Cross-check the item 7/8 counters against the number of child rows in the
   relevant custody group; warn on mismatch, do not silently correct.
7. Apply the annual values; do not prorate by birth month.
8. Use the 2026 table only for 2026; year-stamp the data.
