# Employer obligations

## 1. Demand the form

1. Demand a 101 from every employee who is not a daily worker or foreign worker
   (regulation 2(c)).
2. Demand it within 7 days of start/first pay, and again on 1 January.
3. Do not accept the previous year’s form as the current year’s declaration; the
   declaration is annual.
4. If the employee does not provide it, withhold at the maximum rate and document
   the demand.

## 2. Verify before applying a benefit

The employer is not a rubber stamp. The 2026 circular’s נספח א requires the
employer/software to:

- enforce all mandatory fields;
- enforce the attachment list (ID + ספח; passport + visa for non-ID employees;
  oleh certificate; NII disability-child approval; form 1312א; discharge
  certificate; form 119; divorce/support judgment; assessor approval for
  “פרוד/ה”; coordination approval);
- validate that the employee actually meets the conditions (e.g. item 5 requires
  retirement age or disability; item 9 requires the other parent dead/unknown;
  item 11 requires the other parent not claiming the same points);
- reject/return an invalid form rather than applying the credit.

The employer may rely in good faith on a complete declaration, but a knowingly
false benefit exposes the employer to assessment and penalties. When in doubt,
send the employee to the assessor.

## 3. Compute and withhold correctly

1. Apply the ordinary monthly table if the form is valid and the employee claims
   the credits here.
2. Apply the **maximum rate** if:
   - no form;
   - the “other income” section is blank;
   - the employee ticked “I receive credits elsewhere” (ה item 8);
   - the income is a second job / partial / daily and no coordination applies.
3. Pay the withheld tax to the Tax Authority on the statutory schedule (s. 164,
   166).
4. Issue form 106 to the employee by **31 March** and file form 126 by **30
   April** (s. 166(b); form-106 service page).
5. On termination, issue form 106 at the end of the employment.

## 4. Keep the form and its attachments

- Paper: keep the signed original + copies of attachments.
- Electronic: keep the locked PDF, the attachments, the employee’s confirmation
  and **all previous versions**, in a retrievable archive
  ([[source/taxes-electronic-101-rules-2026]] §ב.2.י–ב.2.יא).
- Retention period: see [[law/retention]] (7 years from the end of the tax year,
  or 6 years from filing, whichever is later).
- Do not keep data longer than the retention/privacy rules allow; do not use the
  101 data for marketing or transfer it to advertisers.

## 5. Do not misrepresent a form-filler as an approved system

The 2026 circular permits employers to replace the annual paper form only with
software **approved by the Tax Authority**, using employee password identification,
an explicit declaration, employer e-signature and a versioned archive. A
browser-only tool that produces a PDF:

- is a **form filler / printer**, not an approved electronic-101 system;
- must not claim to satisfy regulation 2(e) or the circular;
- should tell the employee to sign and deliver the PDF to the employer, or to
  enter the data into the employer’s approved system if the employer uses one.

## 6. Privacy and security

- The employer is the controller of the employee’s 101 data (ID, children, health
  fund, income).
- The legal basis is the statutory withholding duty, not consent; still, the
  employer must provide a privacy notice and secure the data.
- Access should be limited to payroll/HR; paper copies should be locked; digital
  copies should be access-controlled and encrypted at rest where feasible.
- See [[law/privacy-consent]].

## 7. What the employer must *not* do

- Do not apply a credit the employee did not claim.
- Do not apply a credit after the triggering event ended (child turned 18, spouse
  started working, employee ceased to be a resident).
- Do not withhold less than the table without an assessor approval/coordination.
- Do not discard prior versions after an update.
- Do not send the form or its data to a third party (including the app vendor)
  without a legal basis and the employee’s knowledge.
