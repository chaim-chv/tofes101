# Deadlines

## 1. The three dates

| Event | Deadline | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New employee | **within 7 days of starting work, or on the first payday, whichever is earlier** | regulation 2(a)(1) |
| Continuing employee | **1 January** of each tax year | regulation 2(a)(2) |
| Any change in the declared particulars | **within 7 days of the change** | regulation 2(d) |
| Employer’s demand | same dates as the employee’s duty | regulation 2(c) |

“7 days” is a week, not 7 working days; the regulation says “תוך שבוע ימים”.
The first-payday alternative is an **outer** limit, not an extension: if the
employee starts on 1 January and is paid on 31 January, the form is due by
8 January, not 31 January.

## 2. Annual renewal

- The declaration is annual. A form from the previous tax year is **not** valid
  for the new year; the employer may not rely on it.
- The 2026 electronic-101 circular repeats the rule: the employee fills at the
  start of work and again on 1 January (unless the Director approved otherwise).
- Regulation 2(e) allows the Director to exempt an employer that proves it
  computes payroll and tracks family status/children/spouse declarations by
  machine. The exemption is employer-specific and requires approval; it is not a
  general “digital employers don’t need annual forms” rule. The 2026 circular
  implements this for approved electronic systems.
- Pensioner administrative exemptions (70+, etc.) are summarised in
  [[law/who-must-file]].

## 3. Mid-year changes

1. The employee must notify the employer **within 7 days** of the change
   (marriage, divorce, birth, death, change of address, new job, etc.).
2. The employer should obtain a **new form** (or a new version in an approved
   electronic system), not a scribble on the old one.
3. The payroll adjusts the withholding from the change; the annual return / form
   106 reconciles the whole year. For part-year marital status, the credits are
   prorated by months (s. 41 for a spouse who was married part of the year).
4. A change that increases credits (birth, aliyah, disability) should be applied
   as soon as the document is provided; a change that reduces them (spouse starts
   working, child turns 18) must not be ignored retroactively without checking the
   annual computation.

## 4. Late or missing form

- The employer must withhold at the **maximum rate** (47%) if the form is missing
  or the other-income section is blank (regulation 5(a)).
- The employee can claim the credits later in the annual return or a refund claim
  (up to 6 years), but the monthly withholding will have been too high.
- A form that is incomplete/invalid is treated like a missing form for withholding
  purposes; the 2026 electronic circular says an uncompleted process is a “טופס
  לקוי” ⇒ maximum withholding.
- No fixed administrative fine on the employee for lateness was found. A false
  declaration is an offence under the Ordinance. **Unverified:** the exact
  sanction for an employer that fails to demand the form.

## 5. Other payroll dates that touch the 101

| Document | Deadline | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form 106 (annual employee statement) | **31 March** after the tax year | shows the employee what was withheld; used for refunds |
| Form 126 (employer’s annual report) | **30 April** after the tax year | section 166(b) of the Ordinance |
| Form 116 / online coordination | before the payroll relies on it; current tax year only | without it, second job/other income ⇒ max withholding |
| Refund claim | up to **6 years** from the end of the tax year | the fallback when credits were not applied |

## 6. Calendar example (2026)

- Started work 12 February 2026 → form due **19 February 2026** (or first payday
  if earlier).
- Continued from 2025 → new form due **1 January 2026**.
- Married on 3 August 2026 → notice + updated form by **10 August 2026**.
- Child born 20 December 2026 → notice by **27 December 2026**; the credit is
  annual for 2026 (birth year), not prorated by month.
